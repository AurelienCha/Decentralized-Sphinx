Our results show that decentralizing Sphinx header construction is feasible and secure under reasonable assumptions, but several conceptual and practical limitations remain.
These do not undermine the contributions of the present work, but highlight challenges for real-world deployment.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Decentralized Path Selection.}
While DSphinx decentralizes header construction, it remains client-centric since he still selects the path and derives hop-by-hop shared secrets.
This allows malicious clients to bias route selection or mount classical deanonymization attacks such as (N-1)-attacks~\cite{oram2001peer}, reducing anonimity of honest clients.
A natural direction for future work is to decentralize path selection and key derivation by distributing these tasks among STTPs to reduce client trust and enabling a better trust model for practical deployments.

DSphinx remains secure as long as at least one STTP is honest and not all nodes on a path are compromised.
If an adversary controls both a node and an STTP involved in the packet, they could link integrity weights to that packet, enabling integrity forgery and weakening unlinkability.
Although we proposed a mitigation, future work should explore stronger integrity schemes that are fully resilient to any form of collusion.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Privacy-Preserving Policy Enforcement.} 
A major challenge in bringing decentralized networks such as mixnets and peer-to-peer into practical use is the lack of accountability and policy-enforcement mechanisms that do not undermine privacy~\cite{oram2001peer}.
Because Sphinx reveals almost no information to the nodes in the path, verifying client legitimacy or enforcing policy is extremely difficult.  
We believe that decentralized packet construction, such as DSphinx, opens a path toward generating privacy-preserving credentials or zero-knowledge proofs that can be validated at any node without revealing routing or identity information.  
Developing such integration remains an open and promising research direction.
