\todo{check this paragraph}The main limitation of our work lies in the integrity construction, which introduces a central bottleneck in the protocol. The integrity tag $\mathcal{C}$ is computed as a pseudorandom weighted sum of header chunks, where the weights are derived from iterated hashes of shared secrets. Consequently, the shares obtained during path selection must first be reconstructed at a central entity in order to compute the corresponding hash values, and are then redistributed to the STTPs so that they can compute partial DSphinx headers. Since each STTP holds only a share of a shared secret, it cannot independently derive the hash values required for the integrity computation. 

Designing an integrity mechanism that can be computed directly from secret shares would eliminate this limitation and enable a fully distributed workflow. Such an improvement could allow STTPs to jointly sample the path and construct partial headers in a single interaction round, thereby integrating path selection naturally into the decentralized header construction. 
Exploring alternative integrity mechanisms that support distributed computation while preserving the cryptographic guarantees of DSphinx therefore remains an important direction for future work.

\subsubsection{Privacy-Preserving Policy Enforcement.}

A major challenge in deploying decentralized networks such as mixnets and peer-to-peer systems is the lack of accountability and policy enforcement mechanisms that do not compromise user privacy~\cite{oram2001peer,diaz2007accountable, naylor2014balancing,mirkovic2008building, backes2014backref, xu2012accountable}. 
Because Sphinx intentionally reveals almost no information to nodes along the path, verifying client legitimacy or enforcing network policies is inherently difficult. 
Decentralized packet construction mechanisms such as DSphinx may provide a foundation for addressing this challenge by enabling the generation of privacy-preserving credentials or zero-knowledge proofs that can be verified by intermediate nodes without revealing routing information or client identities. 
Developing such mechanisms and integrating them with decentralized header construction remains an open research direction.

\subsubsection{Performance Evaluation and Latency Analysis.}

Although we provide an asymptotic analysis of computational complexity (Appendix~\ref{app:complexity}) and an empirical evaluation of header randomness (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} and Appendix~\ref{app:emp_results}), a comprehensive performance evaluation remains necessary. 
In particular, future work should include detailed benchmarks assessing the latency and computational overhead introduced by STTP interactions, network delays, and additional cryptographic operations. 
Such measurements will be essential for evaluating the practicality and scalability of decentralized header construction in large-scale deployments.
