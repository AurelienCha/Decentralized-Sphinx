\section{Introduction}

Mix network, or mixnet, is an overlay network of servers, called mixnodes, that prevent an adversary from correlating senders with receivers~\cite{chaum-mix,cypherpunk-remailer,piotrowska2017loopix,nym-network-whitepaper,danezis2003mixminion, van2015vuvuzela,mixmaster-spec,chaum2016cmix}. 
Mixnets achieve this through layered encryption where only the exit node
can see the destination of a packet, and by adding delays to messages inside the different mixnodes in order to mitigate timing analysis attacks. 
Additionally, unlike Tor~\cite{onion-routing96} where all packets sent by a user follow the same path for the entire session (circuit-based), in mixnets each packet follows a different routing routing path (packet-based). 
These two techniques ensure that mixnets are resilient against a strong adversary who observes the entire inputs and outputs of the network, typically called a Global Passive Adversary (GPA).
During the last few decades several systems have been proposed in the literature and few of them have been implemented.

Recently, Nym Technologies a mixnet-based company is being commercialized and proposing a mixnet network for services to be integrated with their network for a fee. 
Their network is based on Loopix~\cite{piotrowska2017loopix}. 
Clients of these services are then allowed to use the Nym mixnet by using
Nym credentials (based on the Coconut credential~\cite{coconut}) that
clients can construct after getting a certificate of payment for a specific
service that is to be used through the Nym network.
However, nothing prevents clients to exploit a valid credential to attempt
to access services for which they have not paid for.
Such misuse would only be detected, and handled by discarding the packet,
at the mixnet’s exit gateway, which results in wasted resources for the intermediate nodes.
This illustrates how authentication and dealing with fake packets are
particular difficult problems in anonymous communication networks where the mixnodes, due to layered encryption (cf. Sphinx
packet~\cite{sphinx}), do not know the traffic type or the final service a
client is communicating with, and where the final IP address is only known at the exit gateway.

In this paper we present a scheme to create Sphinx headers in a
decentralized way based on trusted third parties, while ensuring that these
parties learn nothing about the destination or even the route itself.
Having a decentralized scheme offers greater flexibility for real-world
deployment and facilitates ongoing improvements of the technology and its
features.  We argue that a decentralized approach will simplify the
creation of credentials and zero-knowledge proofs that demonstrate the
client has actually used an authorized destination as the final
destination, thus enabling mixnet implementations that address the
aforementioned challenges regarding authentication and fake packet
injection in a privacy-preserving and efficient manner.
