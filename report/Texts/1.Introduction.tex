\section{Introduction}

Mix network, or mixnet, is an overlay network of servers, called mixnodes, that prevent an adversary from correlating senders with receivers~\cite{chaum-mix,cypherpunk-remailer,piotrowska2017loopix,nym-network-whitepaper,danezis2003mixminion, van2015vuvuzela,mixmaster-spec,chaum2016cmix}. 
While various techniques, such as shuffling, re-ordering, and introducing fake packets, are used in mixnets to resist a powerful adversary who is capable of observing all network inputs and outputs, the use of the Sphinx packet format, introduced by Danezis et al.~\cite{sphinx} in 2003, is perhaps one of the most important.
First, Sphinx packets have a uniform size and structure, which prevents adversaries from linking senders to recipients using traffic analysis techniques. This property is known as \textit{bit-wise unlinkability}. Additionally, packets are encrypted in layers, ensuring that only the current relay node can decrypt its portion of the routing information and learn the previous and next hops. Finally, the Sphinx packet format includes integrity checks, so if a malicious server modifies the routing information, the packet will be rejected by the next honest node.

Sphinx is used in various Anonymous Communication Networks (ACNs), including Nym~\cite{nym-network-whitepaper}, Katzenpost~\cite{infeld2025echomix}, as well as the Lightning Network~\cite{poon2016bitcoin}, all for the same purpose: to prevent adversaries from linking the originator to the destination of the packet.
Sphinx packets are constructed by the clients, who encode all the routing information such as the IP addresses and the necessary cryptographic elements in the header of the packet to be attached with the payload which includes the content of the message.
While this simplifies routing, it opens the door for malicious clients who could bias the routes and not follow the randomized path selection in order to reduce the overall anonymity provided by the mixnet~\cite{benguirat2022mixnet}. 
Additionally, in systems like Nym, where users must pay a subscription fee to route messages for a specific application, users authenticate using anonymous credentials. 
However, nothing prevents them from using the network to route traffic for an unauthorized application they have not paid for, potentially overloading the network.

In this paper we present a novel scheme to create Sphinx headers in a decentralized way based on trusted third parties, while ensuring that these parties learn nothing about the route, destination or cryptographic secrets. 
Having a decentralized scheme offers greater flexibility and robustness for real-world deployment and facilitates ongoing improvements of the technology and its features.  
We argue that a decentralized approach will simplify the creation of anonymous credentials and enable enforcement of access control policies without compromising privacy.

\noindent Our work makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We propose a decentralized approach to Sphinx header construction;

  \item We provide a Python implementation of our header construction to support a comprehensive evaluation of our approach in Nym or other mixnets;

  \item We evaluate the unlinkability property of our decentralized construction by analyzing the randomness of the headers using the NIST SP 800-22 statistical test suite. 
  We show that our construction passes all the tests that the original Sphinx passed. 
  Additionally it succeeds in three tests where the original Sphinx packet format fails.
\end{itemize}
