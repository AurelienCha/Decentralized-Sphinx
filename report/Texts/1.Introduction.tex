\begin{comment}
A mix network (or mixnet) is an overlay network of servers, called mixnodes, designed to prevent adversaries from correlating senders with receivers~\cite{chaum-mix,cypherpunk-remailer,piotrowska2017loopix,nym-network-whitepaper,danezis2003mixminion, van2015vuvuzela,mixmaster-spec,chaum2016cmix}. 
Unlike Tor~\cite{onion-routing96}, where all packets follow the same path throughout a session (circuit-based), mixnets route each packet independently along different paths (packet-based), making traffic analysis significantly harder.
To further strengthen resistance against traffic analysis, mixnets employ techniques such as packet shuffling, reordering, and fake packets injection, aiming to resist even powerful adversaries capable of observing the entire network. 
\end{comment}
Sphinx packet format, introduced by Danezis and Goldberg~\cite{sphinx} in 2009, has been used in  various Decentralized networks such as the Lightning Network~\cite{poon2016bitcoin} and mixnet-based systems for example the Nym network~\cite{nym-network-whitepaper} and Katzenpost~\cite{infeld2025echomix}. In such decentralized  networks where a client sends a message to a recipient via multiple hops, sphinx packet format provides crucial privacy and security properties. However, Sphinx packets are constructed by the client, who encodes all the routing information such as IP addresses and cryptographic elements within the packet header.
First, Sphinx ensures that all packets are of fixed size and structure, preventing correlation through traffic analysis.
It also employs layered encryption, ensuring that each mixnode learns only its immediate predecessor and successor, not the entire path of the packet. Additionally, it includes integrity checks to detect tampering. 
If any part of the routing data is altered by a malicious mixnode, the next mixnode will reject the packet.

However, this design does not protect against malicious clients who bias the routes and not follow the routing protocol either to reduce the overall anonymity provided by the mixnet~\cite{benguirat2022mixnet}, access unauthorized services, launch (N-1)-attack etc.


In this paper, we present a novel scheme to create Sphinx headers in a decentralized manner using trusted third parties, while ensuring that these parties learn nothing about the route, destination, or cryptographic secrets. 
Having a decentralized scheme offers greater flexibility and robustness for real-world deployment and facilitates ongoing improvements of mixnet-based systems.
