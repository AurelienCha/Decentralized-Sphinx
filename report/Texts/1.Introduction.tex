\section{Introduction}

Mix network, or mixnet, is an overlay network of servers, called mixnodes, that prevent an adversary from correlating senders with receivers~\cite{chaum-mix,cypherpunk-remailer,piotrowska2017loopix,nym-network-whitepaper,danezis2003mixminion, van2015vuvuzela,mixmaster-spec,chaum2016cmix}. 
They achieve this by adding delays to messages inside the different mixnodes in order to mitigate timing analysis attacks.

Additionally, unlike Tor~\cite{onion-routing96} where all packets sent by a user follow the same path for the entire session (circuit-based), in mixnets each packet follows a different routing routing path (packet-based). 
These two techniques ensure that mixnets are resilient against a strong adversary who observes the entire inputs and outputs of the network, typically called a Global Passive Adversary (GPA).

Recently, Nym Technologies a mixnet-based company is being commercialized and proposing a mixnet network for services to be integrated with their network for a fee. 
Their network is based on Loopix~\cite{piotrowska2017loopix}. 
Clients of these services are then allowed to use the Nym mixnet by using Nym credentials (based on the Coconut credential~\cite{coconut}) that they can construct after getting a certificate of paying for a specific service to use the Nym network.
However, nothing prevents clients to exploit a valid credential to access services for which they have not paid for.
Such misuse would only be detected, and handled by discarding the packet,
at the mixnet’s exit gateway, which results in wasted resources for the intermediate nodes.
This is a particular difficult problem in anonymous communication networks
where the mixnodes, due to layered encryption (cf. Sphinx
packet~\cite{sphinx}), do not know the traffic type or the final service a
client is communicating with, and where the final IP address is only known at the exit gateway.


In this paper we present a scheme that creates the Sphinx header in a decentralized way based on trusted third parties while ensuring that these parties learn nothing about the destination or the path.
Having a decentralized scheme offers greater flexibility for real-world deployment and facilitates ongoing improvements of the technology and its features.
We think a decentralized approach could simplify the creation of credentials and zero-knowledge proofs that demonstrate the client has actually used an authorized destination as the final destination (which is encrypted under multiple layers).


\noindent Our work makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We propose a decentralized approach to Sphinx header construction;
  \item We evaluate the unlinkability property of both the original Sphinx packet and our decentralized construction by assessing their randomness using the NIST SP 800-22 statistical test suite.
\end{itemize}


We highlight related work and motivation in Section~\ref{sec:related}, then we specify and justify our system model in Section~\ref{sec:sys_model}, where we also describe the considered threat model. 
We then present our scheme that decentralize the creation of the Sphinx headers in Section~\ref{sec:scheme} and the evaluation of our proposed solution in Section~\ref{sec:eval}. 
Finally we conclude and discuss future work in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}
