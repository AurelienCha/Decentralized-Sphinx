\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

% Scheme and security
In this paper, we proposed a decentralized construction of Sphinx packet headers, in which the client outsources header generation to a set of semi-trusted third parties (STTPs). 
Our scheme preserves the core cryptographic guarantees of Sphinx by ensuring that no coalition of STTPs, as long as at least one remains honest, can recover routing information or shared secrets. Through a game-based security analysis, we established integrity-forgery resistance and unlinkability under different adversarial models, and we identified the conditions under which collusion between STTPs and mixnodes may become problematic.
Although this residual vulnerability could be mitigated by additional client-side computation, designing stronger homomorphic integrity mechanisms remains an open challenge. % TODO keep or add

% Implementation
To evaluate our construction, we implemented a Python prototype and applied the NIST SP-800-22 statistical test suite to the resulting headers.  
Those experiments show that our decentralized variant produces headers that are at least as indistinguishable from random bitstrings as those generated by the original Sphinx protocol.  
In some tests, our construction even performs significantly better, suggesting that decentralization does not degrade the statistical quality essential for unlinkability.

% Decentralized path selection
In its current form, our protocol remains partially client-centric since the client continues to select the route and derive the hop-by-hop shared secrets.  
Consequently, a malicious client may still bias paths or attempt (N-1)-attacks.

% Privacy-preserving policy enforcement
Beyond decentralizing trust, our approach opens avenues for practical enhancements of anonymous communication systems.  
In particular, distributed header construction may serve as a foundation for privacy-preserving policy enforcement or legitimacy verification at intermediate hops.  
Such mechanisms could allow mixnodes to detect and discard unauthorized traffic early, reducing resource consumption and mitigating denial-of-service threats, without compromising user anonymity.
