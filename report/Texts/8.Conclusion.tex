\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

% Scheme and security
In this paper, we presented DSphinx, a decentralized construction of Sphinx packet headers, in which the client outsources header generation to a set of semi-trusted third parties (STTPs). 
Our scheme preserves the core cryptographic guarantees of Sphinx by ensuring that no coalition of STTPs, as long as at least one remains honest, can recover routing information or shared secrets. Through a game-based security analysis, we established integrity-forgery resistance and unlinkability under different adversarial models, and we identified the conditions under which collusion between STTPs and network nodes may become problematic.
Although this residual vulnerability could be mitigated by additional client-side computation, designing stronger homomorphic integrity mechanisms remains an open challenge. 

% Implementation
To evaluate our construction, we implemented a Python prototype and applied the NIST SP-800-22 statistical test suite to the resulting headers.  
Experiments show that our decentralized variant produces headers that are at least as indistinguishable from random bitstrings as those generated by the original Sphinx protocol.  
In five out of the 15 tests, our construction performs significantly better
than Sphinx, suggesting that decentralization does not degrade the
statistical properties essential for unlinkability.

% Decentralized path selection
In its current form, our protocol remains partially client-centric since the client continues to select the route and derive the hop-by-hop shared secrets.  
Consequently, a malicious client may still bias paths, attempt (N-1)-attacks and violate exit policy strategies.  
A major remaining step toward full decentralization is therefore the removal of client control over path selection and secret derivation, ideally by distributing these responsibilities among STTPs without exposing routing information.

% Privacy-preserving policy enforcement
Beyond decentralizing trust, our approach opens avenues for practical enhancements of anonymous communication systems.  
In particular, distributed header construction may serve as a foundation for privacy-preserving policy enforcement or legitimacy verification at intermediate hops.  
Such mechanisms could allow network nodes to detect and discard unauthorized traffic early, reducing resource consumption and mitigating denial-of-service threats, without compromising user anonymity.

% Final words
In summary, our decentralized header construction preserves Sphinx's essential privacy and integrity guarantees while reducing reliance on a fully trusted client. 
Although several challenges remain before reaching a fully decentralized
and deployable design, we believe that our work provides a significant step
towards more flexible, robust, and privacy-preserving decentralized
networks such as mixnets, and to support continued progress in secure anonymous communication systems.
