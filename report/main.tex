\begin{abstract}
%
Sphinx is a packet format widely
used in decentralized networks such as Nym, Katzenpost, and the Lightning network with the goal to achieve network privacy. % Nym, Katzenpost (Mixnets), and Lightning (Blockchain)
It protects against network % necessary to specify 'wrap resistance' ?
adversaries who aim to identify communicating parties by correlating the input % "correlating input and output" is bit blur
with the output traffic by providing strong security and privacy properties% such as integrity protection, wrap resistence and unlinkability.
However, Sphinx does not protect against malicious clients who exploit the centralised packet construction in order to launch attacks such as denial-of-service (DoS) and $N-1$ attacks, 
or to deviate from the prescribed routing protocol.
% Maybe reformulate this sentence to not highlight the "protection against biased route"

In this paper, we propose a decentralized variant of the
Sphinx packet format that distributes header construction among multiple  semi-trusted third parties.  We provide a formal analysis of the main security properties of our decentralized Sphinx packet format in the presence of network adversaries, semi-trusted Third Parties, and malicious 
clients.  Additionally, we implement our scheme in Python and evaluate the unlinkability property using the NIST SP 800-22 statistical test suite.  Our empirical
results suggest that our decentralized scheme achieves unlinkability,
passing more statistical tests than the original Sphinx format.
\end{abstract}
